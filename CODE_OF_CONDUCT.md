# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume good faith. Argue with the code, not the person.

## What we expect

- **Technical disagreement is welcome; contempt is not.** "This is wrong because
  X" is the point of the project. "You are an idiot" is not an argument.
- **Say what you measured.** Confident claims without evidence waste everyone's
  time, and this project cares more than most about the difference between a
  measurement and an assertion.
- **Be willing to be wrong in public.** Retracting a claim is a contribution.
  Nobody here will think less of you for it.
- **Respect people's time.** Search before filing, include a reproduction, and
  answer the question you were asked.
- **Treat everyone the same** regardless of experience, background, identity, or
  how good their English is. A first-time contributor's bug report is worth the
  same care as a maintainer's.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, intimidation, or personal attacks, in the tracker or anywhere else
  the project's name is attached.
- Demeaning comments about a person or group — including about someone's
  competence as a stand-in for their identity.
- Sexualised language or imagery, or unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption: derailing threads, reopening settled decisions without
  new evidence, or bad-faith argument.

## Scope

This applies in the repository — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit
messages, code and comments — and anywhere someone is representing the project.

## Reporting

Report conduct problems privately to **hello@trithypha.dev**. Reports are read by
the maintainers and kept confidential. Tell us what happened, where, and whether
you would like us to act or simply to be aware.

We will respond, and we will tell you what we did. If a report concerns a
maintainer, say so and it will be handled by someone else.

## Enforcement

Depending on severity and pattern:

1. **A private word** — most things end here, and usually as a misunderstanding.
2. **A public correction**, with a request to change the behaviour.
3. **A temporary ban** from interaction with the project.
4. **A permanent ban** for serious or repeated breaches.

We would rather de-escalate than punish, and we will not pretend a warning was a
ban or a ban was a warning. Enforcement decisions are recorded privately so they
are applied consistently.
